## What is an urban district?

In MultiRoofs, a **district** is an area with characteristics that set it apart from other parts of the city: its dominant land use, building types and functions, or specific planning rules. A district describes how similar buildings, uses and construction types are grouped within a city, often as a result of its history, geography or planning decisions.

Districts help local authorities collect and structure the information they need to activate rooftops: where rooftop space is available, which functions are sustainable there, and which incentives fit the local property owners.

## How districts are defined

The MultiRoofs district categories draw on two concepts used in European land statistics, such as the EU [LUCAS survey](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/lucas):

- **Land cover** describes what is physically on the ground. In MultiRoofs, this mainly distinguishes built structures with roofs from natural areas.
- **Land use** describes the socio-economic purpose of the land, such as residential, industrial, agricultural or recreational.

Each district is described by three parameters:

- **Main function:** mixed use, residential housing, industrial and services, recreation and nature, or unused.
- **Population density:** defined by each city, either qualitatively (low, medium, high) or with numerical thresholds.
- **Spatial boundaries:** the actual area on the map. A city can have several districts of the same category with different boundaries.

## The six district categories

| Urban district | Main function | Population density | Possible sub-categories |
|---|---|---|---|
| **City centre** | Mixed use: housing combined with commercial activities and services | Very high | Historical or modern city centre |
| **Sub-centre** | Mixed use | Medium | Neighbourhood and village sub-centres |
| **Residential** | Residential housing | High | Urban residential |
| | | Medium | Urban green residential |

| **Industrial and services** | Industry and manufacturing, energy facilities (power plants, grid substations, wastewater treatment plants), business parks and office areas | Low to medium | |
| **Green area** | Recreation and nature | Low | Rural areas, parks, sports facilities and other open areas |
| **Unused** | None: unused or abandoned land that is not considered nature | Very low | |

## Districts and rooftop potential

Each district type has its own mix of buildings, owners and challenges. City centres are dense and often constrained by heritage. Residential areas range from low-rise neighbourhoods to compact apartment blocks. Industrial districts have large roofs that may have structural limits. Green and unused areas play a role in ecological or transitional planning.

Matching rooftop functions to the district improves their impact, for example by prioritising energy functions in industrial zones, or social and green functions in residential areas.

Cities can refine the six categories with local sub-districts and additional parameters, such as dominant building types, owner profiles or specific planning rules. This keeps the framework comparable across cities while adapting it to local needs.

- A district groups areas with a similar land use, density and building stock.

- Each city sets its own density thresholds and district boundaries.
- The district lens shows where rooftop capacity matches local needs.
