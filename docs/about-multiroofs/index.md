# About MultiRoofs 

Cities in North-West Europe need more space for green areas, water storage, renewable energy, housing and social facilities, but land is finite. Rooftops cover large areas of every city, yet most of them remain unused or serve a single function. MultiRoofs explores how public authorities can turn this overlooked space into multifunctional rooftops that make cities more liveable and resilient.

**Mission.** MultiRoofs transforms urban rooftops into accessible, biodiverse and inclusive spaces that make cities more liveable and resilient.

**Vision.** MultiRoofs sees roofscapes as drivers of collective urban transformation, powered by open data, digital tools and community engagement.

## Why multifunctional rooftops

A rooftop can do more than one thing at a time. Vegetation cools the roof and the surrounding area, which also makes solar panels more efficient. Water retention reduces flood risk and keeps plants alive in dry periods. When functions are combined carefully, these **golden combinations** deliver more value than each function on its own.

MultiRoofs classifies rooftop functions with a seven-colour system originally developed for the City of Rotterdam: green (nature and biodiversity), blue (water), yellow (energy), red (social and community), orange (mobility), purple (densification) and grey (utilities and infrastructure). See [Rooftops and buildings](../rooftops-and-buildings/index.md) for details.

The potential of rooftops is well known. However, cities hold back due to split responsibilities across departments, lack of shared ways to prioritise actions, owners face financial, practical and legal barriers, and available data is not translated into decisions. MultiRoofs addresses these systemic barriers.

## The MultiRoofs methodology

MultiRoofs is a decision-support methodology for public authorities. It brings together three perspectives that are usually treated separately, known in Dutch as *Moeten, Kunnen, Willen*:

- **Need (Moeten):** the urban challenges a city or district faces, such as heat,   flooding, energy demand, housing shortage or lack of green space. See
  [Urban challenges](../urban-challenges/index.md).
- **Can (Kunnen):** the physical, technical and regulatory capacity of specific roofs, buildings and districts. See
  [Rooftops and buildings](../rooftops-and-buildings/index.md).
- **Want (Willen):** the interests, constraints and decision logic of building owners, users and institutions. See
  [Owners and incentives](../owners-and-incentives/index.md).

The methodology is applied through an 8-step rooftop activation roadmap that guides public authorities from identifying priorities to designing incentives and pilots. It is generic at the method level and adapted locally: each city applies it with its own data, priorities and regulations.

The digital tools support this process by visualising rooftop potential, allowing scenarios to be compared, and giving departments and stakeholders a shared picture to discuss. It does not make decisions. Political and organisational choices remain with the public authority.

## What the project delivers

MultiRoofs creates, tests and scales three solutions that help public authorities influence how rooftops are used:

1. **A set of tools** to determine what is possible and desirable on specific roofs. There is no single tool. The Multiroofs digital process involves a series of existing and developed applications during the project.
2. **Approaches to identify property owners** who are likely to use their roofs multifunctionally, and the moments when they are most open to it (such as renovation or reroofing).
3. **Incentives** that encourage these owners to act.

These solutions are tested in six pilot actions and then shared through a training programme for 30 additional public authorities across North-West Europe.

## How the project is organised

The project has three work packages:

| Work package | Focus |
|---|---|
| **WP1: Joint strategy** | A transnational framework for multifunctional rooftops: data inventories, shared definitions (rooftop functions, districts, buildings, owners, urban challenges), strategic prioritisation, and action plans for the pilots and the training. |
| **WP2: Pilots and solutions** | Development of the digital twin, owner identification approaches and incentives, tested in the six pilot areas. |
| **WP3: Training and scaling** | A joint training scheme and a community of practice that help other public authorities apply the MultiRoofs approach. |

## Who this is for

The MultiRoofs approach is designed for urban planners and programme managers, asset and portfolio managers, data and GIS teams, and policy officers working on climate, energy, water, biodiversity, housing and urban development.
