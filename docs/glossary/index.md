# Glossary

This glossary defines the terms used across this site. Terms in **bold** within a definition have their own entry.

```{glossary}
:sorted:

8-step rooftop activation roadmap
  The sequence of steps that guides a public authority from identifying its
  priorities to designing incentives and pilots: setting city-wide priorities,
  translating them to districts, setting measurable targets, mapping owners and
  buildings, building a **rooftop passport**, matching opportunities with
  challenges, selecting function combinations, and designing targeted incentives.
  It structures decisions but does not make them. See
  [From priorities to action](../from-priorities-to-action/index.md).

Allocation
  One of the six **incentive characteristics**: how recipients are selected.
  Possible values are competitive, entitlement (anyone who meets the conditions),
  first-come (until the budget runs out) and negotiated (case by case).

Attachment
  One of the six **incentive characteristics**: what the benefits and obligations of
  an incentive are tied to, and therefore what happens when a property is sold.
  Possible values are the person, the project and the property.

Building characteristics
  The features of a building that determine which **rooftop functions** are
  feasible: primary use, **construction era**, roof type, height, materials,
  construction method, location and applicable regulations.

Building footprint
  The outline of a building on the ground, as recorded in a building register or
  cadastre. Combined with **LiDAR**, it can be used to reconstruct a 3D building
  model.

Building information model (BIM)
  A detailed digital model of a single building, typically used in design,
  construction and facility management.

Can
Kunnen
  The second perspective of the MultiRoofs methodology: the physical, technical and
  regulatory capacity of roofs, buildings and districts. Assessed through
  **building characteristics**, **urban districts** and the **rooftop passport**.

CityGML
  An international standard of the Open Geospatial Consortium for describing 3D city
  models, including buildings and their parts.

CityJSON
  A JSON-based encoding of the **CityGML** data model, more compact and easier to
  process. MultiRoofs uses CityJSON as its standard format for 3D building models.

Construction era
  The period in which a building was built. MultiRoofs distinguishes six eras
  (before 1830, 1830–1918, 1919–1945, 1946–1975, 1976–2000, after 2000), which
  indicate typical roof forms, materials and structural capacity.

Coordinate reference system (CRS)
  The system that defines how coordinates relate to locations on the Earth. Datasets
  with different coordinate reference systems must be converted before they can be
  combined.

Cost-benefit comparison
  The comparison of a rooftop option with conventional ground-level alternatives
  for the same challenge, counting non-financial constraints (structure, heritage,
  ownership, administration, maintenance) as costs.

Derived grouping
  A category of incentives that follows automatically from their characteristics,
  such as property-based, publicly funded or competitive incentives. One incentive
  can belong to several derived groupings.

Descriptive service
  A function of a **digital twin** that shows the current state of its physical
  counterpart, for example through maps, dashboards or indicators.

Digital model
  A digital representation of an object with no automatic data exchange with it.
  Data is entered manually, and the model is typically used for design or offline
  analysis.

Digital shadow
  A digital representation that receives data automatically from its physical
  counterpart, but does not send information back to it automatically.

Digital twin
  A digital representation of a physical object, system or place that exchanges data
  with its real counterpart in both directions, so that it reflects the counterpart's
  state and can be used to act on it. In urban practice, the term is often used more
  broadly, including for **digital models** and **digital shadows** of cities. See
  [Digital twins](../digital-twins/index.md).

Digital twin cycle
  The sequence of stages through which a **digital twin** connects the physical and
  digital worlds: create, interact, aggregate, analyze, insight and decision.

Five dimensions of a digital twin
  A model describing a **digital twin** through its physical entity, virtual entity,
  data, services and connection.

Funding source
  One of the six **incentive characteristics**: who pays for the incentive.
  Possible values are community, private and public (European, national, regional or
  municipal).

GDPR
General Data Protection Regulation
  The European Union regulation on the protection of personal data. It applies, for
  example, to ownership data about individuals.

Geographic information system (GIS)
  Software and data used to store, analyse and display information linked to
  locations.

Golden combination
  A combination of **rooftop functions** that reinforce each other, delivering more
  value than each function alone. For example, green and blue roofs together cool
  the roof and retain water.

Incentive
  Any measure that changes an owner's decision to activate a rooftop, including
  plans, regulations, advice, contracts, access to equipment and financial support.
  Each concrete incentive has one **instrument type** and is described by six
  **incentive characteristics**. See
  [Incentives](../owners-and-incentives/incentives.md).

Incentive characteristics
  The six properties that describe how an **incentive** works: **nature**,
  **function**, **funding source**, **allocation**, **attachment** and
  **project stage**.

Incentive function
  One of the six **incentive characteristics**: what the incentive changes for the
  project, such as reducing upfront costs, raising additional capital, de-risking or
  building capacity and awareness.

Incentive nature
  One of the six **incentive characteristics**: the kind of lever an incentive uses.
  Possible values are contractual, financial (including fiscal), informational,
  organisational and regulatory.

Instrument type
  What an **incentive** is. MultiRoofs distinguishes nine families of instrument
  types: planning, regulatory, informational, contractual or governance, use or
  access, equity or collective finance, debt, fiscal and direct transfer
  instruments. Each concrete incentive has exactly one type, chosen with a
  nine-question decision tree.

Internet of Things (IoT)
  Networks of connected sensors and devices that collect and exchange data, such as
  weather stations or water level sensors.

Land cover
  What is physically present on the ground, such as buildings, vegetation or water.

Land use
  The socio-economic purpose of land, such as residential, industrial, agricultural
  or recreational.

Level of detail (LoD)
LoD
  How much of a building's geometry a 3D model contains. LoD1 models represent
  buildings as blocks with flat tops; LoD2 models include roof shapes.

LoD2.2
  A level of detail that represents roof shapes in enough detail to analyse each
  roof surface separately, including features such as dormers. MultiRoofs uses
  LoD2.2 models for rooftop analysis.

LiDAR
  Light Detection and Ranging. A remote sensing technique, usually airborne, that
  measures distances with laser pulses and produces a **point cloud** of the
  surface. Used to reconstruct 3D building models.

Metadata
  Information that describes a dataset, such as its content, coverage, coordinate
  reference system, update frequency, licence and provenance.

Model-based digital twin
  A **digital twin** whose models are based on known principles, such as physical
  laws or engineering rules.

Model-free digital twin
  A **digital twin** whose models learn patterns from data, for example with machine
  learning, without an explicit model of the system.

Moments of opportunity
  Moments when building owners are most open to rooftop activation, because they
  already plan work on their building: new construction, renovation, reroofing,
  maintenance or energy upgrades.

MultiRoofs framework
  The framework that connects what roofs can offer (summarised in **rooftop
  passports**) with what the city needs (a local menu of urban priorities), and
  then confronts the match with what stakeholders want.

Need
Moeten
  The first perspective of the MultiRoofs methodology: the **urban challenges** a
  city or district faces.

Open data
  Data that anyone can access, use and share, usually under an open licence.

Owner type
  A category of building owners with similar motivations and decision processes.
  MultiRoofs distinguishes ten owner types, from private individual homeowners to
  institutional investors. See
  [Property owners](../owners-and-incentives/property-owners.md).

Persistent identifier
  A stable, unique identifier for a building or other object, used to link datasets
  that describe the same object.

Point cloud
  A set of points in space, each with coordinates and sometimes other attributes,
  typically produced by **LiDAR**.

Predictive service
  A function of a **digital twin** that estimates future states or tests what-if
  scenarios.

Prescriptive service
  A function of a **digital twin** that suggests actions, for example by comparing
  scenarios against objectives.

Project stage
  One of the six **incentive characteristics**: when an incentive acts. Possible
  values are pre-investment, investment and operation.

Roofer
  An open-source software tool developed at TU Delft that automatically reconstructs
  **LoD2.2** building models from **building footprints** and **LiDAR**.

Rooftop activation
  Turning an unused or single-purpose roof into a roof that serves one or more
  **rooftop functions**.

Rooftop function
  A use of a roof, classified in MultiRoofs with the **seven-colour system**.

Rooftop passport
  A summary of all available data about a roof, describing its spatial, technical,
  organisational and financial conditions.

Scenario
  A possible combination of rooftop functions on a set of roofs, used to compare
  options and their contribution to local targets.

Sensor
  A device or method that captures data about the physical world. Sensors can be
  built-in (already part of the object), added (installed for the purpose) or
  **virtual**.

Seven-colour system
  The classification of **rooftop functions** used in MultiRoofs, originally
  developed for the City of Rotterdam: green (nature and biodiversity), blue (water
  retention and management), yellow (energy transition), red (social and community),
  orange (mobility), purple (densification) and grey (utilities and
  infrastructure). See
  [Rooftop functions](../rooftops-and-buildings/rooftop-functions.md).

Strategic prioritisation framework
  The method developed by the MultiRoofs partner cities to decide where and how to
  act, based on three pillars: clarifying priorities, comparing costs and benefits,
  and designing incentives.

Sub-challenge
  A specific problem within one of the ten **urban challenges**. MultiRoofs
  distinguishes 47 sub-challenges.

Synchronisation
  The alignment of a **digital twin** with the current state of its physical
  counterpart. How often it is needed depends on the purpose of the twin.

Urban challenge
  A pressure that cities face and that multifunctional rooftops can help reduce or
  adapt to. MultiRoofs distinguishes ten urban challenges. See
  [Urban challenges](../urban-challenges/index.md).

Urban district
  An area of a city with characteristics that set it apart, such as its dominant
  land use, density, building types or planning rules. MultiRoofs uses six
  categories: city centre, sub-centre, residential, industrial and services, green
  area, and unused. See
  [Urban districts](../rooftops-and-buildings/urban-districts.md).

Urban heat island
  The effect that makes cities warmer than their surroundings, caused by sealed
  surfaces, limited greenery, dense building and urban heat sources.

Virtual sensor
  A method that estimates a feature that is hard to measure directly from other
  data. For example, the solar irradiation of a roof can be simulated from its
  shape, orientation and surroundings.

Want
Willen
  The third perspective of the MultiRoofs methodology: the interests, constraints
  and decision logic of building owners, users and institutions, and the incentives
  that influence them.
```
