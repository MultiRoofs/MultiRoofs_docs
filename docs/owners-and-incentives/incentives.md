## What is an incentive?

In MultiRoofs, an incentive is **any measure that changes an owner's decision to activate a rooftop**. This is broader than money: it includes plans, regulations, advice, contracts, shared access to equipment, and financial support.

MultiRoofs distinguishes between:

- **incentive types**, such as a grant, a voucher or an advisory service, and
- **concrete incentives**: the measures that actually exist in a region, such as the green roof grant of a given city.

Each concrete incentive is described by **one instrument type**, which says what it is, and by **six characteristics**, which say how it works. It is also linked to the region that implements it and to the rooftop colours it supports.

This classification follows the [Rooftop Activation Ontology](https://alejandro3500.github.io/Rooftop_activation_ontology/), which formalises the knowledge developed in MultiRoofs.

## Why one classification is not enough

Incentives are often grouped by what they achieve, for example person-based financing, property-linked financing, reducing upfront costs, or raising additional capital. These groups overlap. A grant paid before the works starts reduces upfront costs, is tied to a project and may be publicly funded, all at once. MultiRoofs therefore classifies incentives by instrument type only, and treats every other property as a separate characteristic. The familiar groups are then derived from
these characteristics (see [Derived groupings](#derived-groupings)).

## Choosing the instrument type

Every concrete incentive has **one** instrument type, and the nine families exclude each other: an incentive cannot be both a grant and a tax credit. If a programme combines several instruments, for example a grant plus free advice, it is described as several incentives, one per instrument.

To choose the family, answer these questions in order and stop at the first "yes". The order resolves cases where more than one answer seems to fit.

1. Does it act through spatial planning: a plan, strategy, public programme or zoning designation? → **Planning instrument**
2. Is it a binding rule, or a mandatory obligation to report information? → **Regulatory instrument**
3. Does it provide information, advice, recognition or tools? → **Informational instrument**
4. Is it a condition, charge or agreement negotiated with each recipient: a contract clause, developer charge, ESCO agreement, offset of other obligations, or public-private partnership? → **Contractual or governance instrument**
5. Does it give access to rooftop space, equipment or services without buying them: renting or leasing, group purchasing, crowdsourcing? → **Use or access instrument**
6. Is capital collected from many citizens through crowdfunding, or pooled in a fund that finances several projects? → **Equity or collective finance instrument**
7. Must the money be repaid: loan, credit line, bond? → **Debt instrument**
8. Is it delivered through the tax system: tax credit, reduction, exemption, reduced VAT, levy? → **Fiscal instrument**
9. Does the recipient receive non-repayable money or value: grant, subsidy, tariff, voucher? → **Direct transfer instrument**

## The nine instrument families

The last column lists every characteristic fixed by the type, including those it inherits from its parent type. Characteristics that are not listed are not fixed by the type: they depend on how each concrete incentive is designed, and are added when it is described. For example, the type "Grant" fixes the attachment and the project stage, but the funding source and the allocation depend on the specific grant. See [Characteristics of an incentive](#characteristics-of-an-incentive).

### Planning instruments

Measures that act through spatial planning.

| Type | What it is | Fixed by the type |
|---|---|---|
| Planning or policy | A plan, strategy or policy document that sets objectives or priorities for rooftop activation. | Organisational nature only |
| Programme or zoning approach | A programme or zoning rule that designates areas, targets or requirements, such as a green area factor. | Organisational nature only |

### Regulatory instruments

Binding rules.

| Type | What it is | Fixed by the type |
|---|---|---|
| Regulation | A binding rule that requires, permits or forbids rooftop activation, such as a mandatory green or solar roof for new buildings. | Regulatory nature only; mandating or constraining |
| Disclosure | An obligation to report or publish information about a building or its rooftop. | Regulatory nature only |

### Informational instruments

Measures that provide information, advice, recognition or tools.

| Type | What it is | Fixed by the type |
|---|---|---|
| Advisory service | Technical, legal or financial advice to assess and prepare a project. | Informational nature only; building capacity or awareness; pre-investment stage |
| Information or engagement | A campaign, event or outreach activity that raises awareness. | Informational nature only; pre-investment stage |
| Label or recognition | A certification, label or award for activated rooftops. | Informational nature only; operation stage |
| Tools | A tool to identify, assess or plan rooftop activation. | Informational nature only; pre-investment stage |
| ↳ Digital platform service | An online platform that connects owners, providers, investors or users, or manages the process. | Informational nature only; pre-investment stage |
| ↳ Mapping tools | A map or cadastre showing the activation potential of rooftops. | Informational nature only; pre-investment stage |

### Contractual or governance instruments

Conditions, charges or agreements set case by case.

| Type | What it is | Fixed by the type |
|---|---|---|
| Contractual condition | A clause in a lease, sale, concession or permit that requires or rewards rooftop activation. | Contractual nature; negotiated allocation |
| Developer charge or fee | A one-off development or impact charge that is reduced or waived when a rooftop is activated. | Contractual nature; negotiated allocation |
| ESCO or sustainability agreement | An energy service company or similar provider designs, finances and operates the installation, and recovers its investment from the savings or revenues. | Contractual and financial nature; negotiated allocation; attached to the property |
| Offset or deduction mechanism | A mechanism that lets rooftop activation count towards other obligations, such as required green space, parking or stormwater retention. | Contractual nature; negotiated allocation |
| Public-private partnership | A long-term arrangement in which a public authority and private partners share financing, delivery and risks. | Contractual and organisational nature; raising additional capital; public and private funding; negotiated allocation |

### Use or access instruments

Access to rooftop space, equipment or services without buying them.

| Type | What it is | Fixed by the type |
|---|---|---|
| Renting or leasing | Use of rooftop space or equipment through a rental or lease, for example an owner leasing the roof to a solar operator. | Financial nature; reducing upfront costs |
| Group purchases and collective projects | Pooling the demand of several owners to buy jointly or deliver a shared project. | Organisational nature; reducing upfront costs and transaction costs |
| Crowdsourcing | Collecting ideas, rooftop offers or volunteer work from the public. | Organisational nature; reducing upfront costs |

### Equity or collective finance instruments

Capital collected from many people or pooled to finance several projects.

| Type | What it is | Fixed by the type |
|---|---|---|
| Crowdfunding, crowdlending and crowd equity | Many small contributions from people, usually through an online platform, as donations, loans or shares. | Financial nature; raising additional capital; community funding |
| Fund | A pool of capital that finances several projects. | Financial nature; raising additional capital |
| ↳ Funding vehicle | A fund held in a dedicated legal entity, such as a special purpose vehicle. | Financial nature; raising additional capital |
| ↳ Investment fund | A fund taking equity or quasi-equity positions for a financial return. | Financial nature; raising additional capital |
| ↳ Revolving fund | A fund that reinvests repayments and returns in new projects. | Financial nature; raising additional capital |
| ↳ Steward-owned fund | A fund in steward ownership, with profits bound to its mission. | Financial nature; raising additional capital; community funding |

### Debt instruments

Money that must be repaid.

| Type | What it is | Fixed by the type |
|---|---|---|
| Loan | Capital lent and repaid over time. | Financial nature; investment stage |
| ↳ On-bill financing | A loan repaid through the utility bill. | Contractual and financial nature; attached to the property; investment stage |
| ↳ On-bill repayment | A loan repaid through the utility bill, with capital from a third-party lender. | Contractual and financial nature; attached to the property; investment and operation stages |
| ↳ On-tax financing | A loan repaid through a surcharge on the property tax bill. | Financial and fiscal nature; attached to the property; investment stage |
| ↳ Property-linked loan | A loan secured on the property, whose balance transfers with ownership. | Financial nature; attached to the property; investment stage |
| ↳ Revolving credit | A credit line that can be drawn, repaid and drawn again. | Financial nature; attached to the person; investment stage |
| Bond | A debt security issued to investors. | Financial nature; investment stage |
| ↳ Green bond | A bond whose proceeds are earmarked for environmental projects. | Financial nature; raising additional capital; private funding; investment stage |
| ↳↳ Green project bond | Issued for specific projects whose risk investors bear directly. | Financial nature; raising additional capital; private funding; investment stage |
| ↳↳ Green revenue bond | Repaid from the revenues, fees or taxes generated by the projects. | Financial nature; raising additional capital; private funding; investment stage |
| ↳↳ Secured green bond | Backed by collateral from the projects. | Financial nature; raising additional capital; private funding; investment stage |
| ↳↳ Standard green use-of-proceeds bond | With recourse to the issuer's full balance sheet. | Financial nature; raising additional capital; private funding; investment stage |

### Fiscal instruments

Measures delivered through the tax system.

| Type | What it is | Fixed by the type |
|---|---|---|
| Tax credit | An amount deducted from the tax owed by a taxpayer who invests in rooftop activation. | Fiscal nature; entitlement allocation; attached to the person |
| ↳ Citizen micro-investment tax credit | A tax credit for small citizen investments, for example through a cooperative. | Fiscal nature; entitlement allocation; attached to the person |
| Tax or VAT incentive | A tax reduction, exemption or reduced VAT rate granted automatically for eligible works. | Fiscal nature; entitlement allocation; attached to the person |
| Fiscal measure | A levy, fee or rate adjustment that makes non-activated roofs more costly than activated ones, such as an annual stormwater fee based on roof area. | Fiscal nature |

### Direct transfer instruments

Non-repayable money or value.

| Type | What it is | Fixed by the type |
|---|---|---|
| Grant | A non-repayable sum to fund a defined project. | Financial nature; attached to the project; investment stage |
| ↳ Blended finance programme | A grant combining public money with private capital. | Financial nature; public and private funding; attached to the project; investment stage |
| ↳ Call for projects or competitive grant | A grant awarded through a call for projects, ranked against criteria. | Financial nature; competitive allocation; attached to the project; investment stage |
| ↳ Pre-financed grant | A grant paid fully or partly before the works. | Financial nature; reducing upfront costs; attached to the project; pre-investment and investment stages |
| Subsidy | A recurring payment or price support during operation. | Financial nature; attached to the project; operation stage |
| ↳ Feed-in tariff | A guaranteed price per unit of energy fed into the grid. | Financial nature; improving operating economics; attached to the project; operation stage |
| Voucher | A voucher of fixed value, redeemable for goods or services such as a feasibility study. | Financial nature; reducing upfront costs; attached to the person; investment stage |

The three grant subtypes are the only types that can be combined. A grant awarded through a call and paid in advance is both a competitive grant and a pre-financed grant.

### Telling similar types apart

Some incentives seem to fit more than one instrument type. The table below gives examples of these borderline situations and shows which of the existing types to choose. In most cases, the decision depends on a single detail, such as whether a charge is paid once or every year, or whether the owner receives a loan or manages the fund itself. When in doubt, follow the order of the [decision tree](#choosing-the-instrument-type): the first question answered with "yes" determines the type.

| Situation | Classify as | Rather than | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| A green area factor or other zoning designation | Programme or zoning approach | Regulation | It applies to designated areas through planning, not to all buildings. |
| A building code obligation for all new buildings | Regulation | Programme or zoning approach | It is a binding rule that applies everywhere, not to a designated area. |
| An annual stormwater or sealing fee based on roof area | Fiscal measure | Developer charge or fee | It is a recurring levy, not a one-off charge on a development. |
| A one-off charge on a development or permit, reduced if the roof is activated | Developer charge or fee | Fiscal measure | It is charged once, as a condition of the development or permit. |
| Citizens lending money to a project through a platform (crowdlending) | Crowdfunding, crowdlending and crowd equity | Loan | The capital comes from many citizens, which makes it collective finance. |
| A loan an owner receives from a revolving fund | Loan | Revolving fund | What the owner receives is a loan. |
| The revolving fund itself, as a programme | Revolving fund | Revolving credit | It is a pool of capital financing several projects, not a credit line for one owner. |
| An owner leases the roof to a solar operator | Renting or leasing | ESCO agreement | The owner gives access to the roof; the operator does not repay itself from the owner's savings. |
| A provider finances and operates the installation and is repaid from the savings | ESCO agreement | Renting or leasing | The provider recovers its investment from the savings or revenues of the installation. |
| A grant paid per kWh produced | Feed-in tariff | Grant | It is a payment during operation based on energy produced, not a sum for a defined project. |

## Characteristics of an incentive

Beyond its type, each incentive is described by six characteristics. Some values come with the instrument type (see the tables above); the others are added for each concrete incentive.

**Nature: what kind of lever does it use?** Contractual, financial (including fiscal), informational, organisational or regulatory. An incentive can combine several natures, except planning, regulatory and informational instruments, which have only one.

**Function: what does it change for the project?** An incentive can have several functions.

| Function | The incentive... |
|---|---|
| Reducing upfront costs | lowers the capital the owner must mobilise at the moment of investment |
| Improving operating economics | improves recurring revenue or lowers recurring costs during operation |
| Raising additional capital | brings in third-party capital that would not otherwise reach the project |
| De-risking | transfers or absorbs technical, financial or performance risk |
| Reducing transaction costs | lowers search, negotiation, procurement or administrative effort |
| Building capacity or awareness | increases the knowledge, skills or motivation of owners and intermediaries |
| Coordinating actors | aligns the decisions of several parties |
| Mandating or constraining | obliges or forbids an action |
| Stimulating the market or innovation | develops supply, demand or technology beyond a single project |

**Funding source: who pays for it?** Community, private or public. Public funding is specified as European, national, regional or municipal when known. A co-funded incentive has several sources. Regulations and planning instruments usually have none.

**Allocation: how are recipients selected?** Exactly one of:

| Allocation | Recipients are selected... |
|---|---|
| Competitive | by ranking applications against criteria, with no guarantee of award |
| Entitlement | automatically, if they meet the conditions |
| First-come | in order of application, until the budget or quota is used up |
| Negotiated | case by case, with terms agreed between provider and recipient |

**Attachment: what happens if the property is sold?** Exactly one of:

| Attachment | The benefit or obligation... |
|---|---|
| Person | stays with the person or company that received it |
| Project | is tied to the project and ends when the project ends |
| Property | transfers to the next owner or occupant |

**Project stage: when does it act?** One or more of:

| Stage | Moment |
|---|---|
| Pre-investment | before the investment decision: awareness, feasibility, design, preparation |
| Investment | at construction or installation, when the capital is spent |
| Operation | after commissioning: use, maintenance, revenue, repayment |

## Derived groupings

Because characteristics are recorded separately, the groupings used in practice follow automatically from them. One incentive can belong to several groupings.

| Grouping | Applies to any incentive that... |
|---|---|
| Financial incentive | has a financial or fiscal nature |
| Non-financial incentive | is a planning, regulatory or informational instrument |
| Regulatory incentive | has a regulatory nature |
| Contractual or governance incentive | has a contractual or organisational nature |
| Publicly funded incentive | has a public funding source at any level |
| European incentive | has a European funding source |
| European grant | is a grant with a European funding source |
| Competitive incentive | is allocated competitively |
| Person-based incentive | is attached to the person |
| Property-based incentive | is attached to the property |
| Pre-investment incentive | acts at the pre-investment stage |
| Reducing upfront cost incentive | has the function of reducing upfront costs |

## Example

A city offers a **green roof grant**: a non-repayable sum covering part of the cost of a green roof, paid after the works until the annual budget is used up.

- **Type:** questions 1 to 8 of the decision tree are "no", and question 9 is "yes": a grant.
- **Fixed by the type:** financial nature, attached to the project, investment stage.
- **Added characteristics:** reducing upfront costs, regional funding, first-come allocation.
- **Rooftop colour supported:** green.
- **Derived groupings:** financial incentive, publicly funded incentive, reducing upfront cost incentive.

## Incentives, rooftops and owners

Each concrete incentive is linked to the rooftop colours it supports, for example a grant for green or blue roofs. Separately, MultiRoofs records how suitable each incentive type is for each owner type, as a degree between 0 and 1 that a city can adjust to its own context. These links make it possible to trace how an incentive reaches owners, which roofs they activate, and which urban challenges those roofs address. The 8-step roadmap groups instruments more broadly into financial, advisory, regulatory and communication measures. In terms of the nine families, financial measures cover the debt, direct transfer, equity or collective finance, fiscal and use or access instruments; advisory and communication measures are informational instruments; and regulation covers the regulatory and planning instruments. Contractual or governance instruments combine elements of several groups.

## What works in practice

The MultiRoofs partner cities shared which incentives they use and which work best:

- **Money alone is rarely enough.** Financial incentives work best when combined with advisory services, clear communication, accessible tools and stable rules.
- **Advisory services make a difference.** Many owners, especially homeowner associations and small landlords, struggle to interpret technical guidance, compare long-term costs and benefits, or coordinate decisions among several parties.
- **Predictability matters.** Large owners plan years ahead. Short-lived programmes and changing rules erode trust, while stable funding and regulation increase willingness to invest.
- **Gaps exist.** Existing incentives do not reach all owner types equally.

```{admonition} Key takeaways
:class: important
- An incentive is any measure that changes an owner's decision, not only money.
- Each concrete incentive has one of nine instrument types, chosen with a nine-question decision tree.
- Six characteristics describe how it works: nature, function, funding source, allocation, attachment and project stage.
- Familiar groupings such as "property-based" or "publicly funded" follow from these characteristics, so one incentive can belong to several.
- The most effective approach combines financial support with advice, communication, tools and stable rules.
```
