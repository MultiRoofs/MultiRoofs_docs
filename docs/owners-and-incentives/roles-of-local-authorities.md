## Four perspectives on government

Local authorities do more than regulate. In rooftop activation, they also act as partners, facilitators and connectors. MultiRoofs describes these roles using a framework from the Netherlands School of Public Administration (NSOB), developed by Van der Steen, Scherpenisse and Van Twist.

The framework places four perspectives along two axes: whether the government works from the inside out (from its own organisation) or from the outside in (from society), and whether it is oriented towards values or results.

| Perspective | Orientation | What the authority does |
|---|---|---|
| **Legitimate government** (public administration) | Inside out, value-oriented | Sets clear and lawful conditions: planning rules, policies that include rooftop functions, and transparent procedures. This gives owners and investors a stable basis to act. |
| **Performing government** (new public management) | Inside out, result-oriented | Delivers concrete results: pilots, programmes and permitting processes. It coordinates projects, monitors progress and leads by example on public buildings. |
| **Cooperating government** (network governance) | Outside in, result-oriented | Works with private, civic and institutional partners to reach goals it cannot reach alone, through agreements, partnerships and group approaches. |
| **Responsive government** (societal resilience) | Outside in, value-oriented | Supports bottom-up initiatives, community energy and civic creativity, and opens channels for local ideas. |

These perspectives exist side by side. An authority moves between them depending on the context, the partners involved and the goals of the project.

## Concrete roles

| Role | Perspective | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Policy maker** | Legitimate | Embeds rooftop functions in planning norms and regulations. |
| **Project manager** | Performing | Coordinates delivery, solves practical bottlenecks and ensures measurable outcomes. |
| **Public networker** | Cooperating | Connects owners, tenants and partners to enable area-based solutions and collective investment. |
| **Co-creator** | Responsive | Works with civic groups and local initiatives to support experimentation and shared learning. |

Combining these roles allows a local authority to use regulation, delivery capacity, collaboration and community engagement as complementary tools for scaling up multifunctional rooftops.

```{admonition} Key takeaways
:class: important
- Local authorities can act as legitimate, performing, cooperating or responsive governments.
- These perspectives translate into four roles: policy maker, project manager, public networker and co-creator.
- The roles coexist, and authorities switch between them depending on the situation.
```
