## Why owner types matter

Owners decide what happens on their roofs, and they decide in very different ways. Their motivations, time horizons, financial capacity and decision processes vary widely. A homeowner thinks about comfort and personal values, a landlord about return on investment, and a homeowner association must reach a collective agreement. Classifying owners into types helps public authorities understand this diversity and tailor their engagement and incentives to each group.

The MultiRoofs owner typology builds on definitions from the City of Rotterdam's [*Concept Omgevingsprogramma Multifunctionele Daken*](https://indd.adobe.com/view/acce48c3-9876-406c-bc22-2b23f85429ea)(2022), adapted by the project partners to fit all participating cities.

## The ten owner types

| Owner type | Description | Main driver |
|---|---|---|
| **Private individual homeowners** | Individuals or households who own and live in their home. Decisions are long-term and shaped by comfort, personal values and attachment to the neighbourhood. | Comfort and personal values |
| **Landlords** | Individuals or small companies that rent out residential or mixed-use buildings. Investment decisions depend on return on investment and maintenance costs. | Rental income |
| **Homeowner associations (HOAs)** | Collective owners of multi-unit buildings. Decisions are made jointly and often require negotiation and a majority vote. | Collective agreement |
| **Housing corporations / social housing providers** | Non-profit or semi-public organisations managing housing portfolios with a social mission, a long-term view and professional management. | Social mission within budget limits |
| **Municipal real estate owners** | Local authorities owning schools, administrative buildings or public housing. Decisions follow public interest and political priorities. | Policy goals |
| **Corporate real estate owners** | Companies owning the offices, shops or logistics buildings they use. Decisions follow business strategy and cost efficiency. | Business strategy |
| **Industrial property owners** | Companies owning factories, warehouses or large industrial sites. They focus on operational needs, compliance and long-term infrastructure planning. | Operational continuity |
| **Private project developers** | Companies that acquire, develop and then sell or rent real estate, with a short- to medium-term, profit-driven perspective. | Profit |
| **Institutional investors** | Pension funds, investment funds and insurers managing real estate as financial assets, with highly professional decision-making. | Financial return |
| **Religious institutions** | Churches and similar organisations owning places of worship, schools or housing. Often long-term and community-driven, but with limited financial flexibility and frequent heritage constraints. | Community mission |

## Moments of opportunity

Owners are most open to rooftop activation when they already plan work on their building. Typical moments include:

- new construction,
- renovation or reroofing,
- regular maintenance,
- energy upgrades, such as installing solar panels.

Aligning rooftop proposals with these moments lowers costs and barriers, and increases the chance that owners say yes. One of the aims of the MultiRoofs pilots is to develop ways of spotting these moments in advance.

## Local differences

The ten types apply across North-West Europe, but owners within the same type can behave differently from one city to another because of cultural, social and economic factors. In Mannheim, for example, homes often stay in the same family for several generations. In Mechelen, homeowners commonly sell and buy several times within a single generation. The definitions are therefore kept general, and each city should interpret them in light of its local housing market.

:class: important
- MultiRoofs distinguishes ten owner types, from individual homeowners to institutional investors.
- Each type responds to different triggers: comfort, income, social mission, policy goals or profit.
- Owners are most receptive during construction, renovation, maintenance or energy upgrades.
- Owner behaviour varies between cities, so the types must be interpreted locally.
